Random Positioning Machine (RPM) Digital Twin:
Kinematic Transformations, Free-Fall Dynamics, and Scaling Laws for Microgravity Manufacturing

Department of Aerospace Engineering • Manufacturing Technology Specialization
Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), Valiamala, Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala 695547
Abstract — This document details the mathematical, mechanical, and transport foundations of the IIST Random Positioning Machine (RPM) Digital Twin v2.0 (DWG No. 01, 02, 03: MTECH2025). While biological microgravity research enforces an organelle sedimentation threshold of $10^{-3} g_0$, space manufacturing experiments (e.g., Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing, melt pool solidification, and crystal growth) operate under fluid capillary regimes governed by the Bond number ($Bo \ll 1$), Grashof number ($Gr < 10^3$), and Marangoni convection. We formulate the exact kinematic transformations, evaluate the total spatial acceleration field $\vec{a}_{\text{total}}(\vec{r}, t)$, and demonstrate that under low rotational velocities ($\omega \le 1.0\text{ RPM}$), the entire $200 \times 200\text{ mm}$ payload envelope—including onboard IMUs and thermal crucibles—maintains residual centrifugal acceleration below $1.1 \times 10^{-4} g_0$, satisfying the physical criteria for microgravity manufacturing.

1. Kinematic Frame Transformations & Time-Averaged Gravity

Let $\mathcal{F}_{\text{lab}} = \{\vec{e}_X, \vec{e}_Y, \vec{e}_Z\}$ denote the stationary laboratory frame where the static terrestrial gravitational field is $\vec{g}_0 = -g_0 \vec{e}_Z$, with $g_0 = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$. The outer gimbal rotates through angle $\psi$ about the horizontal laboratory shaft ($Y$-axis), and the inner gimbal rotates through angle $\phi$ about its orthogonal gimbal shaft ($Z$-axis). The instantaneous gravity vector projected into the payload frame $\mathcal{F}_{\text{sample}}$ is governed by sequential orthogonal rotation matrices (Yotov et al., 2022):

$$\vec{e}_g(t) = R_Z(-\phi) R_Y(-\psi) \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\sin\phi \cos\psi \\ \sin\phi \sin\psi \\ \cos\phi \end{bmatrix}$$

The cumulative Time-Averaged Simulated Microgravity ($\text{taSMG}$) over operational epoch $T$ is defined as the $L_2$-norm of the integrated unit vector:

$$\text{taSMG} = \left\| \frac{1}{T} \int_{0}^{T} \vec{e}_g(t) \, dt \right\|_2 = \sqrt{\langle g_x \rangle^2 + \langle g_y \rangle^2 + \langle g_z \rangle^2}$$
Theorem 1 (Kim et al., npj Microgravity 2026 — Ergodic Ratio)
Periodic harmonic speed ratios $\gamma = \omega_\psi / \omega_\phi \in \mathbb{Q}$ trap the trajectory vector into closed 1D Lissajous loops on the unit sphere $S^2$, inducing severe polar accumulation. The optimal non-harmonic ratio $\gamma = 1.146$ guarantees uniform ergodic coverage across $S^2$ and minimizes the net residual vector magnitude.

2. Volumetric Spatial Acceleration Field & Radius Thresholds

At any arbitrary spatial coordinate $\vec{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ within the payload chamber, the total instantaneous acceleration experienced by a continuum element incorporates terrestrial gravity, centrifugal bias, and Euler angular acceleration (Wadhwa et al., Scientific Reports 2026, Eq. 5):

$$\vec{a}_{\text{total}}(\vec{r}, t) = \vec{g}(t) - \vec{\omega}(t) \times \left( \vec{\omega}(t) \times \vec{r} \right) - \vec{\alpha}(t) \times \vec{r}$$

Where $\vec{\omega}(t) = \omega_\phi \vec{e}_z + \omega_\psi \vec{e}_y$ is the composite angular velocity, and $\vec{\alpha}(t) = d\vec{\omega}/dt$ is the angular acceleration vector limited by motor torque ramping ($\|\vec{\alpha}\| \le 10\text{ rad/s}^2$).

3. Microgravity Manufacturing vs. Biological Scaling Laws

In cellular biology, microgravity is defined by the sedimentation threshold of intracellular organelles (statoliths, $\Delta\rho \approx 500\text{ kg/m}^3$), which enforces a strict condition $a_{\text{centrifugal}} < 10^{-3} g_0$. Under $\omega = 2.0\text{ RPM}$, this restricts the biological specimen radius to:

$$r_{\text{safe}}^{\text{(bio)}} = \frac{10^{-3} g_0}{\|\vec{\omega}\|^2} \approx 2.23\text{ cm}$$

The Manufacturing Paradigm: For manufacturing experiments (Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing, welding, directional solidification, casting, and sintering), the experimental apparatus comprises a molten melt pool, thermal crucible, heating coils, power leads, and onboard IMUs. A $2\text{ cm}$ radius is physically insufficient to house even an IMU. However, in manufacturing, gravity defects are driven by melt pool hydrostatic sag and buoyancy-driven convective turbulence, governed by dimensionless transport numbers:

Definition 1 (Bond Number — Capillary Domination in Manufacturing)
The ratio of gravitational body forces to surface tension capillary forces across a molten metal pool of characteristic radius $L_{\text{melt}}$ (typically $3\text{ to }6\text{ mm}$) with surface tension $\sigma \approx 1.2\text{ to }1.8\text{ N/m}$ is: $$\text{Bo} = \frac{\rho_{\text{metal}} \cdot g_{\text{eff}} \cdot L_{\text{melt}}^2}{\sigma} \ll 1$$ When $\text{Bo} \ll 1$, surface tension completely dominates hydrostatic pressure. The molten bead maintains its shape without dripping or sag, exhibiting true zero-gravity surface morphology even under residual accelerations of $0.05 g_0$.
Definition 2 (Grashof & Rayleigh Numbers — Thermal Buoyancy Suppression)
Buoyancy-induced natural convection in the molten pool is quantified by: $$\text{Gr} = \frac{g_{\text{eff}} \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta T \cdot L^3}{\nu^2}, \qquad \text{Ra} = \text{Gr} \cdot \text{Pr} = \frac{g_{\text{eff}} \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta T \cdot L^3}{\nu \cdot \alpha_{\text{thermal}}}$$ In terrestrial conditions, $\text{Ra} > 10^5$ induces turbulent convective macro-segregation. By attenuating effective directional gravity via the RPM, $\text{Ra} < 10^3$, completely suppressing convective plumes and yielding purely diffusion-controlled dendritic solidification.

4. Payload Envelope Feasibility for the 200 mm CAD Assembly

The IIST manufacturing payload holder (DWG 02, Part 3) is a $200 \times 200 \times 200\text{ mm}$ enclosure ($r = 10.0\text{ cm}$). When operational speed is tuned to the manufacturing regime ($\omega = 0.5\text{ to }1.0\text{ RPM}$):

$$a_c(r=10\text{ cm}, 0.5\text{ RPM}) = (0.0524\text{ rad/s})^2 \cdot (0.10\text{ m}) = 2.74 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m/s}^2 \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5} g_0$$

At $\omega = 0.5\text{ RPM}$, the residual centrifugal acceleration at the outer edge of the $200\text{ mm}$ payload is two orders of magnitude below $10^{-3} g_0$. This confirms that an entire manufacturing sub-assembly with crucible, feed mechanism, and IMU operates well within the microgravity manufacturing boundary.

5. 1D Navier-Stokes Cylindrical Momentum Diffusion

For liquid phases and cooling fluids, the unsteady momentum diffusion induced by gimbal reorientation is modeled at 50 Hz via finite difference discretization (Hasenstein & van Loon, 2015):

$$\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial t} = \nu \left( \frac{\partial^2 u_\theta}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial r} - \frac{u_\theta}{r^2} \right)$$
$$\tau_w = \mu \left| \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial r} - \frac{u_\theta}{r} \right|_{r=R_{\text{chamber}}}$$

The IIST RPM employs a dual-gimbal nested rotation structure requiring continuous 360° unconstrained rotation across two orthogonal axes. Power, real-time closed-loop motor control, and synchronous multi-sensor DAQ telemetry are routed through two commercial 12-channel gold-on-gold slip ring capsules, isolating high-current motor power from microvolt-sensitive telemetry lines.
